Muscles of the Upper Limb

High-yield anatomy notes with origin, insertion, nerve supply, action, and clinical points.

Trunk connections Vertebral column connections Shoulder region Arm muscles Forearm muscles Hand muscles Quick revision

Overview

The muscles of the upper limb are grouped by region. For each muscle, the most exam-relevant details are origin, insertion, nerve supply, action, and clinical correlations.

This page covers all major muscle groups in a clear revision format.

A. Muscles Connecting Upper Limb to Trunk

1. Pectoralis Major

Origin: Clavicular head, sternocostal head, and abdominal head.

Insertion: Lateral lip of the bicipital groove of humerus.

Nerve supply: Medial and lateral pectoral nerves.

Action: Adduction and medial rotation of arm; clavicular head flexes arm.

Clinical note: Used in breast reconstruction; tested by pushing hands against hips.

2. Pectoralis Minor

Origin: 3rd, 4th, 5th ribs.

Insertion: Coracoid process of scapula.

Nerve supply: Medial pectoral nerve.

Action: Draws scapula forward and downward; raises ribs in forced inspiration.

Clinical note: Divides axillary artery into three parts.

3. Serratus Anterior

Origin: Outer surfaces of upper 8 ribs.

Insertion: Medial border of scapula.

Nerve supply: Long thoracic nerve.

Action: Rotates scapula for full abduction and keeps it pressed against chest wall.

Clinical note: Injury causes winging of scapula.

4. Subclavius

Origin: Rib 1.

Insertion: Clavicle.

Nerve supply: Nerve to subclavius.

Action: Protects subclavian vessels from fractured clavicle.

B. Muscles Connecting Upper Limb to Vertebral Column

1. Trapezius

Origin: Occipital bone, ligamentum nuchae, spines of thoracic vertebrae.

Insertion: Lateral third of clavicle, acromion, spine of scapula.

Nerve supply: Spinal accessory nerve; C3, C4 for proprioception.

Action: Elevates, retracts, and depresses scapula; rotates scapula for abduction.

2. Latissimus Dorsi

Origin: Lower thoracic vertebrae, lumbar fascia, iliac crest, lower ribs.

Insertion: Floor of intertubercular groove of humerus.

Nerve supply: Thoracodorsal nerve.

Action: Extension, adduction, and medial rotation of arm.

3. Levator Scapulae

Origin: Transverse processes of upper 4 cervical vertebrae.

Insertion: Superior angle of scapula.

Nerve supply: C3, C4 and dorsal scapular nerve.

Action: Elevates scapula.

4. Rhomboid Major and Minor

Origin: Spines of upper thoracic and lower cervical vertebrae.

Insertion: Medial border of scapula.

Nerve supply: Dorsal scapular nerve.

Action: Retract and elevate scapula.

C. Muscles of the Shoulder Region

1. Deltoid

Origin: Lateral third of clavicle, acromion, and spine of scapula.

Insertion: Deltoid tuberosity of humerus.

Nerve supply: Axillary nerve.

Action: Main abductor of arm; anterior fibers flex and medially rotate, posterior fibers extend and laterally rotate.

Clinical note: Injury causes wasting of deltoid and loss of abduction.

2. Rotator Cuff Muscles

Muscle Origin Insertion Nerve supply Action
Supraspinatus Supraspinous fossa Greater tubercle, upper facet Suprascapular nerve Initiates abduction

The rotator cuff stabilizes the shoulder joint. Supraspinatus tendon is commonly affected in painful arc syndrome.

3. Teres Major

Origin: Lower lateral border of scapula.

Insertion: Medial lip of intertubercular groove.

Nerve supply: Lower subscapular nerve.

Action: Medial rotation and adduction.

D. Muscles of the Arm

Anterior Compartment

All are supplied by musculocutaneous nerve except brachialis, which has dual supply.

>Biceps brachii: Powerful supinator and flexor of elbow; long head rupture causes Popeye sign. >Coracobrachialis: Flexes and adducts arm; pierced by musculocutaneous nerve. >Brachialis: Main flexor of elbow joint; supplied by musculocutaneous nerve and a small radial twig.

Posterior Compartment

Triceps brachii: Main extensor of elbow; long head also extends and adducts shoulder.

Nerve supply: Radial nerve.

Clinical note: Radial nerve may be injured in humeral shaft fractures or with crutch use.

E. Muscles of the Forearm

Flexor Compartment

Mostly median nerve, except flexor carpi ulnaris and medial half of flexor digitorum profundus.

>Pronator teres — pronates forearm. >Flexor carpi radialis — flexes and abducts wrist. >Palmaris longus — flexes wrist, tenses palmar fascia. >Flexor carpi ulnaris — flexes and adducts wrist. >Flexor digitorum superficialis — flexes PIP joints.

Deep Flexors

>Flexor digitorum profundus — flexes DIP joints. >Flexor pollicis longus — flexes thumb. >Pronator quadratus — pronates forearm.

Extensor Compartment

All supplied by radial nerve, mainly posterior interosseous branch in deep layer.

>Brachioradialis — flexes elbow. >Extensor carpi radialis longus and brevis. >Extensor digitorum — extends fingers. >Extensor digiti minimi. >Extensor carpi ulnaris.

Deep Extensors

>Supinator — supinates forearm. >Abductor pollicis longus. >Extensor pollicis brevis. >Extensor pollicis longus. >Extensor indicis.

Wrist drop occurs in radial nerve injury due to loss of wrist and finger extensors.

F. Muscles of the Hand

>Thenar muscles: Abductor pollicis brevis, flexor pollicis brevis, opponens pollicis — recurrent branch of median nerve. >Hypothenar muscles: Abductor digiti minimi, flexor digiti minimi brevis, opponens digiti minimi — deep branch of ulnar nerve. >Adductor pollicis: Deep branch of ulnar nerve; tested in Froment’s sign. >Lumbricals: Flex MCP joints and extend IP joints; lateral 2 by median nerve, medial 2 by ulnar nerve. >Interossei: Dorsal interossei abduct, palmar interossei adduct; all supplied by deep branch of ulnar nerve.

Ulnar nerve injury may cause claw hand with hyperextension at MCP joints and flexion at IP joints.
