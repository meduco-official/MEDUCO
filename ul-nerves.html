Neurovasculature of the Upper Limb

Arteries, veins, and nerves of the shoulder, arm, forearm, and hand.

Overview Arteries Veins Nerves Quick Revision

Chapter Overview

The upper limb is divided into regions such as the shoulder, arm, forearm, and hand. Each region has its own arterial, venous, and neural supply.

Understanding this neurovascular pattern is essential before studying injuries, compression syndromes, and clinical anatomy.

Upper Limb Arteries

The main arterial supply of the upper limb begins as the subclavian artery, which continues as the axillary artery, then the brachial artery, and finally divides into the radial and ulnar arteries.

Shoulder

Main artery: Axillary artery

Branches:

>Superior thoracic artery >Thoracoacromial artery >Lateral thoracic artery >Anterior circumflex humeral artery >Posterior circumflex humeral artery >Subscapular artery

Mnemonic: Save The Lions And Protect Species.

Arm

Main artery: Brachial artery

Branches:

>Profunda brachii artery >Nutrient artery to humerus >Superior ulnar collateral artery >Inferior ulnar collateral artery

Forearm

Main arteries: Radial artery and ulnar artery

Radial branches:

>Radial recurrent artery >Palmar carpal branch >Superficial carpal branch

Ulnar branches:

>Anterior ulnar recurrent artery >Posterior ulnar recurrent artery >Muscular arteries >Common interosseous artery >Dorsal carpal artery >Deep palmar artery >Palmar carpal arteries

Mnemonic: Anatomical Pictures Can Definitely Deeply Please.

Hand

The radial and ulnar arteries form the superficial palmar arch and deep palmar arch.

These arches give rise to arteries supplying the muscles, digits, and joints of the hand.

Upper Limb Veins

Shoulder

Main vein: Axillary vein

Arm

Main veins: Brachial veins

Forearm

Main veins: Basilic vein, cephalic vein, median forearm vein

Hand

The hand has deep veins and superficial veins that form the dorsal venous network.

Nerves of the Upper Limb

The nerves of the upper limb arise from the brachial plexus and include the musculocutaneous, axillary, median, radial, and ulnar nerves.

Nerve Main supply
Musculocutaneous nerve Anterior compartment of arm and skin of lateral forearm
Axillary nerve Deltoid, teres minor, long head of triceps, shoulder joint skin
Median nerve Most anterior forearm muscles, thenar eminence, lateral lumbricals, lateral hand skin
Radial nerve Posterior compartments of arm and forearm, dorsolateral hand skin
Ulnar nerve Flexor carpi ulnaris, medial FDP, hypothenar muscles, interossei, medial hand skin

The brachial plexus is formed by spinal nerve roots C5 to T1.
