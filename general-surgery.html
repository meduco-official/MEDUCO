Subject: General Surgery

Master trauma protocols, operative classifications, post-op complications, and high-yield surgical pathology with these 175 definitive mnemonics.

Trauma & Basic Principles

1. Primary Trauma Survey

🧠 The Mnemonic: ABCDE

Airway (with cervical spine protection), Breathing (oxygenation and ventilation), Circulation (hemorrhage control), Disability (neurological status via GCS), Exposure (environmental control/prevent hypothermia).

2. Trauma/Emergency History

🧠 The Mnemonic: AMPLE

Allergies, Medications, Past medical history, Last meal (critical for aspiration risk), Events leading to injury.

3. Focused Assessment with Sonography for Trauma (FAST)

🧠 The Mnemonic: 4 P's

Perihepatic (Morison’s pouch), Perisplenic, Pelvic (pouch of Douglas/rectovesical space), Pericardial view.

4. Troubleshooting sudden hypoxia in an intubated patient

🧠 The Mnemonic: DOPE

Displacement of endotracheal tube, Obstruction (mucus plug, kinked tube), Pneumothorax, Equipment failure (ventilator disconnect).

Postoperative Care & Complications

5. Causes of Postoperative Fever by Day

🧠 The Mnemonic: The 5 W’s

Wind: Atelectasis/Pneumonia (Days 1–2).
Water: Urinary Tract Infection (Days 3–5).
Walk: Deep Vein Thrombosis / PE (Days 4–6).
Wound: Surgical Site Infection (Days 5–7).
Wonder drugs: Drug-induced fever or IV line infection (Days 7+).

6. Immediate Postoperative Complications

🧠 The Mnemonic: Post-Op PROBS

Pain, Reactionary hemorrhage, Oliguria, Basal atelectasis, Shock/Sepsis.

7. Signs of Acute Compartment Syndrome / Arterial Occlusion

🧠 The Mnemonic: The 5 P's

Pain (out of proportion to injury), Pallor, Pulselessness, Paresthesia, Paralysis.

8. Writing Postoperative Orders

🧠 The Mnemonic: FLAVOR

Fluids (IV rates and types), Labs (morning blood work), Activity restrictions, Vital signs monitoring frequency, Oxygen parameters, Re-evaluate / Nursing instructions.

Abdominal Surgery & Pathology

9. Alvarado Score for Acute Appendicitis

🧠 The Mnemonic: MANTRELS

Migratory right iliac fossa pain (1), Anorexia (1), Nausea/Vomiting (1), Tenderness in RIF (2), Rebound tenderness (1), Elevated temperature (1), Leukocytosis (2), Shift to the left / Neutrophilia (1).

10. Causes of Acute Pancreatitis

🧠 The Mnemonic: I GET SMASHED

Idiopathic, Gallstones, Ethanol, Trauma, Steroids, Mumps, Autoimmune, Scorpion sting, Hypercalcemia/Hypertriglyceridemia, ERCP, Drugs.

11. Glasgow Criteria for Pancreatitis Severity

🧠 The Mnemonic: PANCREAS

PaO2 (< 8 kPa / 60 mmHg), Age (> 55 years), Neutrophils (WBC > 15 x 10⁹/L), Calcium (< 2 mmol/L), Renal function (Urea > 16 mmol/L), Enzymes (LDH > 600 U/L or AST > 200 U/L), Albumin (< 32 g/L), Sugar (Blood glucose > 10 mmol/L).

12. Causes of Abdominal Distension

🧠 The Mnemonic: The 6 F’s

Fat, Fluid (ascites), Flatus, Feces, Fetus, Fulminant mass (tumor).

13. Factors Preventing Surgical Fistula Closure

🧠 The Mnemonic: FETID

Foreign body, Epithelialization of the tract, Tumor / Malignancy, Infection / Inflammatory bowel disease, Distal obstruction.

14. The Surgical Sieve for Differential Diagnosis

🧠 The Mnemonic: VITAMIN CDEF

Vascular, Infective, Traumatic, Autoimmune, Metabolic, Iatrogenic/Idiopathic, Neoplastic, Congenital, Degenerative, Endocrine, Functional.

Clearance of fat from Calot's triangle, Attached only by two structures (Cystic duct/artery), Fundus-down clearance, Exposure of the cystic plate fully visualized.

147. Local Factors Delaying Wound Healing

148. Systemic Factors Delaying Wound Healing

🧠 The Mnemonic: D-I-D-N-O-T

149. Surgical Wound Classification Categories

🧠 The Mnemonic: C-L-E-A-N

Clean (Uninfected), Lightly contaminated (Clean-contaminated), Evident contamination (Contaminated), Abscess present / Dirty (Infected pre-operatively).

🧠 The Mnemonic: B-O-W-E-L

🧠 The Mnemonic: N-E-C

Non-specific systemic signs, Enteric local features (distension, guaiac-positive stools), Clear radiographic changes (Pneumatosis intestinalis, portal venous gas).

167. Neuroblastoma vs. Wilms Tumor Distinctions

🧠 The Mnemonic: T-U-M-O-R

T: Crosses midline = Neuroblastoma.
U: Unilateral / Stays on one side = Wilms Tumor.
M: Mixed calcifications on CT = Neuroblastoma.
O: Old age onset = Wilms (peak 3-4 yrs); Neuroblastoma (peak <2 yrs).
R: Renal distortion profile on IVP = Wilms Tumor.

168. Indications for Operative Intervention in Flail Chest

🧠 The Mnemonic: S-T-A-B

Severe respiratory failure requiring prolonged mechanical ventilation, Trauma deformity of chest wall significant, Failure to manage Pain via medical blocks, Bleeding or progressive loss of lung volume on imaging.

169. Tension Pneumothorax Physical Signs Checklist

🧠 The Mnemonic: T-E-N-S-I-O-N

Tracheal deviation away from affected side, Elevated respiratory distress / Cyanosis, No breath sounds on affected side, Subcutaneous emphysema palpable, Intercostal bulging, Outstanding hyperresonance to percussion, Neck veins distended.

170. Massive Hemothorax Definition & Action Checklist

🧠 The Mnemonic: C-H-E-S-T

Collection of blood >1500 mL immediately drained upon chest tube insertion, Hourly drainage >200 mL for 3–4 consecutive hours, Evident ongoing hemodynamic instability, Surgical exploration warranted (Thoracotomy indicator), Transfusion required aggressively.

171. Signs of Pulmonary Embolism in Surgical Ward

🧠 The Mnemonic: P-U-L-S-E

Pleuritic chest pain sudden onset, Unexplained tachypnea and dyspnea, Loud pulmonic component of second heart sound, Swelling or tenderness in unilateral calf (DVT source), ECG changes showing S1Q3T3 pattern.

172. Wells Criteria Score Components for Pulmonary Embolism

🧠 The Mnemonic: W-E-L-L-S

Wound / Immobilization / Recent surgery within 4 weeks (1.5 points), Evidence / Clinical signs of DVT (3.0 points), Likelihood of alternative diagnosis is lower (3.0 points), Lineage of heart rate > 100 bpm (1.5 points), Spit up blood (Hemoptysis) / Malignancy history (1.0 point each).

173. Esophageal Perforation Presentation

🧠 The Mnemonic: P-E-R-F

Pain localized to chest or retrosternal space immediately after procedure/retching, Emphysema subcutaneous visible or palpable in neck, Radiograph showing pneumomediastinum or pleural effusion, Fever spiking rapidly with tachycardia.

174. Indications for Surgery in Empyema Thoracis

🧠 The Mnemonic: C-L-O-S-U-R-E

Chronic trapped lung non-expandable, Loculated pleural fluid Collections failing chest tube drainage, Organism persistently positive on Gram stain after antibiotic therapy, Severe sepsis ongoing with thick pleural rind, Unsuccessful tube thoracostomy decompression, Rib crowding/Chest wall deformity developing, Evident bronchopleural fistula.

175. Blunt Thoracic Aortic Injury Radiographic Signs

🧠 The Mnemonic: A-O-R-T-A

Arch blurriness / Widened mediastinum (>8 cm on AP supine chest film), Obliteration of the aortic knob contour, Rightward deviation of the nasogastric tube / trachea, Tracheobronchial tree shift (Depression of the left mainstem bronchus), Apical pleural cap of blood.
